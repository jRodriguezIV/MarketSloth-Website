Passive tracking

No signal is announced and abandoned.

Unlike every alert service on the market, MarketSloth's attention doesn't end when the alert is sent. Each opportunity is tracked as a living thing, through its whole life.

  1. Developing

    The engine spots a setup forming. You hear nothing yet — that's the point.

  2. Confirmed

    Conditions line up. One plain-language alert, timestamped twice.

  3. Tracked live

    Target-1 hit, runner guidance, invalidation warnings — as they happen.

  4. Resolved

    Target, stop, or expired. Win or lose, the outcome is recorded.

  5. On the ledger

    Into the public monthly report. Losses first, if anything.

Took the trade? You're told when the first target hits and when the setup dies. Skipped it? You still see how it resolved — that's how you calibrate whether to trust the next one.

Discipline

The only product that tells you to stop.

A brokerage makes money when you overtrade. An alert Discord makes money on hype. Their incentives forbid this feature. Ours require it.

During onboarding, you write your own rules. MarketSloth enforces them at the exact moment willpower fails — and your own history, inside the rules vs. outside them, becomes personal proof of why they exist.

  • Max 3 trades / day
  • Max 2 contracts / position
  • Daily loss cap $150
  • No overnight holds
  • Cool-off after 2 straight losses
  • Profit-off-table reminders

Honest expectation-setting

What MarketSloth deliberately isn't.

  • Not a crystal ball

    It doesn't claim to predict markets and doesn't promise income. Any signal can fail — the invalidation level is printed on every alert precisely because failure is normal.

  • Not autonomous

    Nothing executes without you. Every trade is your decision, in your brokerage account. MarketSloth never touches your money.

  • Not a firehose

    Fewer, better, fully-explained alerts is the explicit design goal. You're drowning in noise, not starved of it.

  • Not a guru

    No courses, no lifestyle photos, no "last chance" countdowns. The ledger speaks; we don't.
